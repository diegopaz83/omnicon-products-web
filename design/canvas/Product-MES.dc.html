OEE: one number for real performance

Overall Equipment Effectiveness multiplies three questions. 100% means only good parts, at full speed, with no unplanned stops.

A

Availability

Was it running when it should?

Lost to unplanned stops: breakdowns, missing material, lost signal.

P

Performance

Did it run at ideal speed?

Lost to slow running and short micro-stops.

Q

Quality

Were the parts good?

Lost to scrap and rework.

74%

An illustrative 8-hour shift: 90% availability × 85% performance × 97% quality. Of 480 minutes paid for, only 356 produced good parts at full speed. The other 124 minutes are the opportunity, and the MES shows where they went.

A day in the plant

  1. 1

    Before the shift

    • Shifts are scheduled
    • Routes set product, line and ideal speed
    • Planned stops are registered
    • Orders load from an Excel template
  2. 2

    During the shift

    • The operator starts an order on a line
    • The MES counts parts and detects stops
    • The operator adds each stop’s reason
    • OEE refreshes every minute
  3. 3

    After the shift

    • Reasons can be corrected until end of day
    • The shift is then closed and locked
    • Results are ready for reporting
